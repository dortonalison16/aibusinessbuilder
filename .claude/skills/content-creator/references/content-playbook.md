# The Content Playbook — What Actually Stops the Scroll

*Rules learned from months of real posting and testing, written for any niche. Read before writing
a week. Every rule here exists because breaking it measurably cost reach.*

## The one idea

**Pull, never push.** People don't stop for a product; they stop for their own life, described so
precisely it feels like you read their mind. Open in a moment they recognize, deliver something
useful, and let the product show up late as the natural next step. Name the broken system as the
enemy, never the reader.

## 1. The first line / first second is 80% of the result

Most viewers decide in about two seconds. A reel that loses them there never gets another chance —
no amount of good content later rescues it.

- **The hook must work alone** — no warm-up ("So today I want to talk about…"), no greeting.
- **On-screen hook: 9 words or fewer** (about 3 seconds of reading). Visible on the very first frame.
- **Lead with the payoff** — often the best line is in your third paragraph. Move it to the top.
- **Concrete beats clever.** The winners are small, physical, specific confessions with a number:
  *"I had 47 tabs open and still couldn't start."* Abstract lines lose: *"Stop overthinking."*

### The hook mix — require this every week

| Shape | Example pattern | Per week |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-test** | "You're doing X the hard way if…" / "If you've ever…" | at least 2 |
| **Experience / timeline** (the owner's real story) | "Three years ago I…" / "It took me two tries to…" | at least 1 |
| **Cold, true number** (never income) | "12 minutes. That's how long…" / "5 things I stopped doing…" | at least 1 |
| **Payoff-first** | open on the result, then how | at least 1 |
| **Specific POV** (role + moment + feeling, under 15 words) | "POV: it's 9:40pm and the kids are finally asleep" | at most 2 |
| **Plain advice opener** ("Here's how to…") | — | at most 1 (it's the weakest shape) |

Never repeat an opening used in the last four weeks (check `Content/history/`).

## 2. Length — each platform rewards the opposite

| Platform | Reel length | Spoken words (talking) | On-screen beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram + Facebook | **under 15 seconds** | about 40 words max; payoff heard by second 6 | 3–5 |
| TikTok + YouTube Shorts | **25–35 seconds** | about 70–90 words | 5–7 |

For a talking reel, write **two cuts**: a short IG/FB cut and a full TikTok/YouTube script. Same
idea, different length. Spoken scripts are **full, natural sentences** ("I spent three years doing
this the hard way, and here's what changed") — fragments belong on screen, not in someone's mouth.

**Second-loop trick:** right after the early payoff, open one concrete gap ("…and the third one
surprised me") and close it in the final beat. Always close it — an unresolved loop is clickbait.

## 3. Formats that earn reach early (0 – ~1,000 followers)

- **Reels are the discovery surface.** Feed posts and stories mostly reach existing followers — at
  a small following they're nearly invisible. Put most of the week into reels until the audience
  grows; use carousels for depth and saves.
- **Silent text-on-screen reels** (text over a clip, image or designed background) are the workhorse:
  cheap to make, readable with the sound off, and in our testing held viewers roughly twice as long
  as talking heads early on.
- **Image reels** — a strong single-image post shipped as an 8–12 second reel. Same copy, far more
  reach than the same image as a feed post.
- **Designed list / comparison reels** — a clean numbered list or a two-column comparison that builds
  row by row. Highly saveable.
- **Copy-this reels** — the caption *is* the product: 3–5 complete, pasteable prompts, steps or
  templates, a "Save this" line, one easy question, and (if they have one) a keyword that unlocks
  something *additional*. On screen: a payoff hook (under 50 characters) → "steal them ↓" → the CTA.
- **Pure-value pieces** — about 3 a week that teach something useful with **no offer and no product
  mention**. CTA is *follow* (plus save/share). They build the audience the offers need.
- **Talking heads** — best for story, opinion and trust once there's an audience. Film yourself or
  use an AI avatar (see recommended tools).

## 4. Captions

- **Line 1 (45–70 characters)** names the payoff or the moment — it's the second hook, shown before
  "…more".
- **If the reel teaches, the caption delivers the whole thing.** Never tease what you could give.
- One short "**Save this** for when…" line on teaching posts.
- **One easy question** to invite comments ("Which one are you doing already?").
- **Hashtags: at most 4 on Instagram and Facebook** (5+ risks being treated as spam and cuts reach).
  TikTok and YouTube can take a few more.
- Emojis at line ends, sparingly. The owner's voice, always.

## 5. Calls to action — one per piece, and it must be real

- Exactly **one** CTA per piece: follow, save, comment a keyword, or link in bio.
- **"Comment WORD" only if a comment-to-DM automation (e.g. ManyChat) is actually live for that exact
  word**, and the DM sends what the post promises. A keyword with no automation behind it teaches
  people that commenting does nothing. Keep the owner's live keywords in `client-config.md` under
  `## Content CTAs` and never invent a new one.
- Where no automation exists: "link in bio", or follow/save.
- Soft-sells are **at most 1 in 5 pieces**. The rest earn the right to sell.

## 6. The idea bank, the story bank, and the feedback file

Three small files make every week better than the last. Create them the first time you plan a week
(if you can write files; otherwise keep them in the Business Profile):

- **`Content/idea-bank.md`** — 30+ specific angles built from the owner's audience, product and
  story, each tagged *value / story / soft-sell*. Rotate through it; mark each angle with the date used.
- **`Content/story-bank.md`** — the owner's real moments, told in their words (the before, the turning
  point, small wins, mistakes). Experience hooks come from here. **Never invent a story, a result or
  a testimonial.** Ask the owner for two or three real moments the first time.
- **`Content/PERFORMANCE_FEEDBACK.md`** — the owner's steering wheel:
  ```
  ## ✅ Working
  - (what's getting views, saves, follows — and why we think so)
  ## ❌ Not working
  - (what flopped)
  ## 📌 Pins (angles to use again next week)
  PIN: the "47 tabs" self-test
  ## Notes
  - (anything else: topics to avoid, a launch coming up, a new product)
  ```
  Read it before planning. Offer to update it when they tell you how a post did. Write each pin on its own
  line starting with `PIN:` — that's what the Auto-Pilot writer looks for.

## 7. Before you hand over a week — the quality check

Run this on every piece and fix anything that fails *before* showing the owner:

- [ ] Hook works alone, is on frame 0, and is 9 words or fewer on screen
- [ ] The week hits the hook mix (2 self-tests, 1 experience, 1 cold number, ≤1 advice opener)
- [ ] No opening repeats the last four weeks
- [ ] Reel lengths match the platform (IG/FB under 15s; TikTok/YouTube 25–35s)
- [ ] One CTA per piece; every keyword matches a live automation
- [ ] At most 4 hashtags on Instagram/Facebook captions
- [ ] **No income claims, earnings figures, "make $X", guarantees or "passive income"** — anywhere,
      including on-screen text. Costs and time saved are fine.
- [ ] Nothing the owner hasn't actually done or said is presented as their experience
- [ ] Soft-sells ≤ 1 in 5; at least 3 pure-value pieces
- [ ] Every line sounds like the owner, not like an ad
